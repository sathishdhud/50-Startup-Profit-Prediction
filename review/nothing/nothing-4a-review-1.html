Nothing Phone (4a) Review: After Two Weeks, Here's Who Should Buy It

By Dhuda Sathish | Updated March 6, 2026
Quick Summary
  • Verdict: A stylish mid-ranger with clean software, but not for heavy gamers.
  • Best For: Design lovers, casual users, and those wanting a clean Android experience.
  • Not For: Mobile gamers, photography enthusiasts needing a telephoto/versatile cam.
  • Key Rival: Motorola Edge 70 Fusion offers faster charging and similar clean OS.

We've been using the Nothing Phone (4a) as our primary device for the past two weeks, and honestly, it's been a mixed bag. On paper, this phone looks like a straightforward upgrade over the Nothing Phone (3a). But after living with it daily, commuting with it, taking photos at parties, and running it through our standard battery of tests, some interesting truths emerged.

The mid-range smartphone market in India has become brutally competitive. You have the POCO F-series offering flagship-level performance, Samsung's A-series providing reliable software support, and Motorola pushing clean Android experiences at aggressive prices. So where does the Nothing Phone (4a) fit in this crowded space?

Here’s what you need to know: The Nothing Phone (4a) is for people who value design and software experience over raw performance numbers. If you're a heavy gamer or need the fastest chip in this price range, look elsewhere. But if you want a phone that feels premium, takes decent photos, and doesn't bombard you with bloatware, this could be your pick.

Reasons to Buy the Nothing Phone (4a)

1. Design That Actually Stands Out

Let's be honest, most smartphones under Rs 35,000 look identical. Black rectangles with camera bumps. Nothing's transparent design language continues to be refreshing in a sea of sameness. The Glyph lighting interface on the back isn't just a gimmick; we found ourselves actually using it for notifications during meetings and as a fill light for video calls.

During our testing, at least six people asked us about the phone. One colleague thought it was a "special edition" device costing much more. That's the power of distinctive design. The transparent back with its arranged components creates visual depth that photographs simply can't capture. In person, it looks like a piece of tech art.

Build quality impressed us too. The phone survived a three-foot drop onto tiled flooring (accidental, not intentional testing) with only a minor scuff on the aluminum frame. The Gorilla Glass 5 on front and back held up well against keys and coins in our pocket test over two weeks.

The transparent back design makes the Nothing Phone (4a) instantly recognizable

2. Display Quality That Punches Above Its Price

The 6.7-inch AMOLED display on the Nothing Phone (4a) is genuinely impressive for this price point. We measured peak brightness at around 1,800 nits in our outdoor testing, which is higher than what Nothing officially claims. In direct sunlight, we had no trouble reading notifications or checking directions.

Color accuracy is another strong point. Our colorimeter tests showed Delta E values under 1.5 in the Natural profile, which means colors appear true-to-life. Watching movies on Netflix and YouTube was enjoyable, with deep blacks and vibrant highlights. The 120Hz refresh rate makes scrolling through Instagram and Twitter feel buttery smooth.

However, we noticed PWM dimming at lower brightness levels. If you're sensitive to screen flicker, you might experience some eye strain during nighttime reading sessions. This isn't unique to Nothing, many AMOLED phones in this range exhibit similar behavior, but it's worth mentioning.

The 120Hz AMOLED panel delivers excellent color accuracy and outdoor visibility

3. Camera System That Handles Most Scenarios Well

The dual 50MP camera setup on the Nothing Phone (4a) exceeded our expectations, with some caveats. Daylight photography is genuinely good. We captured detailed images with accurate colors and good dynamic range. The HDR processing manages to balance bright skies and shadowed foregrounds without introducing unnatural artifacts.

Portrait mode is where this phone surprised us. Edge detection is excellent, even with complex subjects like frizzy hair or glasses. The background blur looks natural, not like an artificial cutout. We compared portrait shots side-by-side with the Samsung Galaxy A55, and honestly, Nothing's output looked more pleasing in several scenarios.

Low-light performance is decent but not class-leading. The main sensor captures usable images at night, but noise reduction can sometimes oversmooth details. We found that Night Mode helps significantly, but you'll need steady hands or a surface to get the best results. The ultrawide camera, unfortunately, shows its limitations in challenging light, producing softer images with visible noise.

Camera Test Results

After 200+ test photos across various conditions, we'd rate the main camera at 8/10 for this price segment. It's not going to replace a Pixel or an iPhone for computational photography, but for social media and everyday memories, it does the job admirably.

Portrait mode edge detection impressed us during testing

4. Nothing OS: Clean Software Experience

In an age where budget phones come loaded with pre-installed apps and aggressive ads, Nothing OS feels like a breath of fresh air. The interface is clean, minimal, and remarkably close to stock Android. During our two-week testing period, we encountered zero pre-installed bloatware, no spam notifications, and no "recommended apps" cluttering our app drawer.

The custom widgets and iconography maintain Nothing's distinctive visual identity without feeling forced. We particularly appreciated the smart drawer feature, which automatically categorizes apps, and the quick settings panel that matches the phone's monochromatic aesthetic. Small touches like these make the daily experience enjoyable.

Nothing has promised 3 years of OS updates and 4 years of security patches for the Phone (4a). While not as generous as Samsung's 4+5 year commitment, it's respectable for this price point. We received two security updates during our testing period, suggesting Nothing is taking software support seriously.

Nothing OS offers one of the cleanest Android experiences in this segment

Reasons to Skip the Nothing Phone (4a)

Now for the honest part of this review. The Nothing Phone (4a) isn't for everyone, and we'd be doing you a disservice if we didn't highlight its limitations clearly.

1. Snapdragon 7s Gen 3: Capable But Not Exciting

Let's address the elephant in the room. The Snapdragon 7s Gen 3 is a mid-range chip, and it shows. In our Geekbench 6 testing, the Phone (4a) scored 1,120 in single-core and 3,340 in multi-core tests. For context, the POCO F6 with Snapdragon 8s Gen 3 scores nearly double in multi-core performance.

What does this mean in real life? Daily tasks like browsing, social media, and messaging fly without issues. The phone handled our 50-tab Chrome test without reloading apps aggressively. But gaming tells a different story. BGMI runs at stable 60fps on balanced settings, but pushing it to HDR causes occasional frame drops during intense scenes. Genshin Impact requires medium settings for playable framerates.

We also noticed thermal throttling after 20 minutes of gaming. The phone doesn't get uncomfortably hot, but performance drops noticeably. If mobile gaming is a priority, we'd recommend checking our list of best gaming phones under Rs 35,000 for better alternatives with Snapdragon 8 series chips or MediaTek Dimensity 8300 Ultra.

Gaming performance is adequate but not exceptional

2. Ultrawide Camera Is Disappointing

While the main camera impressed us, the ultrawide sensor feels like a cost-cutting measure. In good lighting, it captures acceptable images, but step into challenging conditions and the limitations become obvious. We noticed significant softness in corners, noise even in moderate indoor lighting, and color shifts compared to the main sensor.

For a phone priced above Rs 30,000, we expected better consistency across lenses. If you frequently photograph landscapes, architecture, or group shots in varied lighting, this inconsistency might frustrate you. We found ourselves avoiding the ultrawide in anything less than perfect conditions.

Ultrawide camera output shows noticeable softness and noise

3. Missing Features That Competitors Offer

The Nothing Phone (4a) lacks several features that have become common in this price segment. There's no wireless charging, which phones like the Samsung Galaxy A55 offer. The IP54 rating provides basic dust and splash resistance, but it's not the IP67/IP68 water resistance we're seeing on competitors.

Charging speed is another area where Nothing could have done better. The 45W wired charging sounds decent on paper, but in practice, it's slower than what competitors offer. We recorded 0-50% in 22 minutes and full charge in 58 minutes. Compare that to the Motorola Edge 70 Fusion, which offers significantly faster charging speeds in a similar price bracket.

The absence of a telephoto lens is understandable at this price point, but the lack of a dedicated macro mode feels like a missed opportunity. Some competitors offer dedicated macro lenses that can capture interesting close-up shots. The Nothing Phone (4a) relies on digital zoom for close-ups, which doesn't produce the same quality.

4. Battery Life Is Average

The 5,000mAh battery should theoretically provide excellent endurance, but our testing revealed average results. In our standardized battery test (looping YouTube at 50% brightness), the Phone (4a) managed 9 hours and 45 minutes. Real-world usage was better but still not class-leading.

On light usage days (social media, messaging, some photography), we consistently reached bedtime with 25-30% remaining. Heavy usage days with navigation, gaming, and lots of camera use saw us reaching for the charger by 7 PM. If you're a heavy user, you might want to carry a power bank for long days away from outlets.

Beyond the Spec Sheet

If you're a spec-focused buyer who compares benchmark numbers and feature lists, the Nothing Phone (4a) will seem underwhelming. You can get faster chips, better cameras, and faster charging at similar prices from competitors. The value proposition here is about the overall experience, not individual specifications.

Final Verdict: Should You Buy It?

After two weeks with the Nothing Phone (4a), we've developed a clear picture of who this phone is for. Let us break it down simply:

Buy the Nothing Phone (4a) if: You prioritize design aesthetics and want a phone that doesn't look like every other black rectangle. You appreciate clean software without ads and bloatware. You're a casual user who doesn't game heavily. You want good display quality for media consumption. You're willing to pay a slight premium for a unique identity.

Skip the Nothing Phone (4a) if: You're a mobile gamer who needs maximum performance. You want the absolute best camera system in this price range. You need features like wireless charging or IP68 water resistance. You prioritize fast charging speeds. You compare phones purely on specification sheets.

The Nothing Phone (4a) at Rs 31,999 (starting price) sits in an awkward position. On paper, it's outgunned by competitors. But phones aren't spec sheets; they're daily companions. And as a daily companion, the Nothing Phone (4a) offers something increasingly rare: personality. In a market saturated with competent but forgettable devices, that counts for something.
