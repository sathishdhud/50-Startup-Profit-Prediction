Nothing Phone (4) Pro Review: A Bold Gamble That Paid Off?

By Dhuda Sathish • Updated March 20, 2026
Key Takeaways
  • Tactile Upgrade: The metal unibody acts as a passive heat dissipator, keeping the phone cool during sustained loads.
  • Zoom Versatility: The dedicated 3.5x Periscope lens offers a focal length rarely seen under ₹40,000.
  • Efficiency King: The Snapdragon 7 Gen 4 optimization yields surprising battery endurance, outperforming larger capacity rivals.

Every year, the mid-range segment becomes a harder place to stand out. Specs have plateaued, and "good enough" is the standard. Nothing, however, has refused to settle for the grey slab aesthetic. With the Nothing Phone (4) Pro, the company isn't just banking on its transparent aesthetic anymore; it’s making a play for the "premium mid-range" crown by using materials—specifically metal—that competitors abandoned years ago to cut costs.

I’ve been using the Phone (4) Pro as my daily driver for two weeks. While the specifications sheet suggests a standard incremental upgrade, the actual user experience tells a different story. It’s a device that sacrifices wireless charging for structural rigidity and thermal performance. Does that trade-off make sense in 2026? Let's break down the reality of using this phone.

Design & Display: Functional Aesthetics

This is arguably the best-feeling phone in the sub-40k segment. Most manufacturers use plastic frames with a matte coating to mimic metal, but the Phone (4) Pro uses the real deal. The difference is immediately apparent the moment you hold it. It has a cold, reassuring heft (approx 195g) that plastic simply cannot replicate.

However, the design choice has functional consequences. The metal back means wireless charging is physically impossible, a feature even last year's plastic-backed competitors included. But there is an upside: thermal management. During my stress tests, the metal chassis acted as a giant heatsink. While other phones trap heat inside plastic shells, the Phone (4) Pro dissipates it across the back, preventing the throttling that often plagues Snapdragon 7-series chips.

The front hosts a 6.83-inch 1.5K flexible OLED. Unlike standard FHD+ panels, the resolution bump here (450 PPI) makes text incredibly sharp. The 144Hz refresh rate is dynamic, scaling down to 1Hz for always-on display. My only gripe? The curved edges of the glass meet the metal frame a bit sharply, which might annoy users who swipe from the edges often.

The Glyph interface is back, but it's not just for notifications anymore. I used the "Countdown Timer" glyph pattern while cooking—being able to see when my pasta was done without touching a greasy screen is a specific, yet genuinely useful, utility.

Performance: The 7 Gen 4 Sweet Spot

Under the hood sits the Snapdragon 7 Gen 4. On paper, it sits below the flagship 8-series, but in daily use, the gap is barely noticeable. Nothing’s optimization of the OS plays a huge role here. The UI animation latency is virtually non-existent. Apps don’t just open; they snap into view.

I tested Genshin Impact at max settings for an hour. The first 20 minutes were locked at 60fps with zero stutters. Around the 30-minute mark, the back of the phone got warm to the touch—expected with a metal body—but the framerate only dipped to 55fps during heavy particle effects. Crucially, it didn't crash or throttle down to 45fps like some other mid-rangers I've tested recently.

Software is Nothing’s secret weapon. Nothing OS 4.0 is arguably cleaner than stock Android. There are zero duplicate apps. You get a file manager, a calculator, and a gallery. That’s it. No bloatware, no "App Store" clones, and no spammy notifications pushing news feeds.

Cameras: The Periscope Advantage

This is where the "Pro" name justifies itself. Most mid-range phones rely on a main sensor and crop in for "zoom," resulting in potato-quality images. The Phone (4) Pro includes a dedicated 50MP Periscope lens with 3.5x optical zoom.

Why does this matter? Focal length. The 3.5x zoom (approx 80mm equivalent) is the perfect "portrait" length. Instead of cropping a wide image, you get natural compression that flattens facial features beautifully.

Daylight performance: Colors lean towards saturation—typical Nothing style—but detail retention is high. Low-light performance: The Night Mode is aggressive. It brightens shadows significantly, sometimes to a fault, lifting dark areas so much it introduces noise. However, the zoom lens retains clarity better than expected in dim lighting, provided you have steady hands.

The Video Conundrum: While the main camera is great, video is a strange limitation. The Snapdragon 7 Gen 4 is capable of 4K 60fps, but Nothing has limited this phone to 4K 30fps. If you are a content creator prioritizing high-frame-rate video, this omission will be a dealbreaker. For casual usage, however, the stabilization at 4K 30fps is excellent.

Battery Life: The Efficiency Surprise

The 5400mAh battery sounds large, but combined with the efficient chip and software, it delivers absurd longevity. In my standard "office day" test (WiFi on, 150 emails, 2 hours YouTube, social media scrolling), I consistently hit 7.5 to 8 hours of Screen-On Time (SOT).

Charging is handled by 50W wired fast charging. It goes from 0 to 50% in roughly 20 minutes. The full charge takes about an hour. Remember, there is no charger in the box, a trend we unfortunately have to accept.

Who Should Buy This Phone?
This phone is ideal for users who prioritize premium build quality, clean software, and camera versatility. It is a perfect choice for those who want a device that stands out aesthetically and functionally. However, users who strictly require wireless charging or professional-grade 4K 60fps video recording may want to consider alternatives like the OnePlus 15R.

Pros and Cons

Pros
  • Metal Build: Unmatched premium feel in this price segment.
  • Zoom Camera: 3.5x optical zoom is a game-changer for portraits.
  • Battery: Consistent 8hr+ SOT with 144Hz enabled.
  • Software: Nothing OS remains the cleanest Android skin available.
  • Thermals: Metal body dissipates heat effectively.
Cons
  • No Wireless Charging: Lost due to metal back design.
  • Video Limits: Capped at 4K 30fps (no 60fps option).
  • Ultra-Wide: The 8MP ultrawide is just average, lacks detail in corners.
  • USB 2.0: Slow data transfer speeds if you move large files often.

Comparison: Nothing Phone (4) Pro vs OnePlus Nord 4

The OnePlus Nord 4 is the natural rival. Both have metal unibodies and similar price tags. However, the usage philosophy differs. The Nord 4 prioritizes raw charging speed (100W) and a slightly cleaner stock look.

The Nothing Phone (4) Pro wins on versatility. The Periscope camera is the deciding factor. If you take photos of people, pets, or events, the optical zoom on the Nothing creates shots the Nord 4 simply cannot replicate without cropping and losing quality. Furthermore, Nothing's Glyph interface offers utility features (lights for notifications, timers) that OnePlus lacks.

Questions and Answers

Is the metal back slippery?
It has a sandblasted texture that offers decent grip, but it is metal, so it slides off fabric surfaces more easily than a textured plastic or silicone case. A case is recommended for grip.
Can I shoot 4K 60fps video?
No. The camera software is currently capped at 4K 30fps. This is a software limitation, as the hardware technically supports higher frame rates.
Does it support Android Auto?
Yes, it works flawlessly with Android Auto via the USB-C port. Wireless Android Auto is also supported.
How are the speakers?
It has stereo speakers (bottom firing + earpiece). They are loud and clear for podcasts, but lack the bass depth you find in phones with dedicated top-firing speakers.

Overall, this is one of the most balanced and thoughtfully designed mid-range smartphones available right now. It earns a strong recommendation for anyone looking to step out of the generic smartphone cycle.
